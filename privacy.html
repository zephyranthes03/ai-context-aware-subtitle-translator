Privacy Policy — AI Context-Aware Subtitle Translator

Last updated: 2026-08-16

This extension does not operate a server, and the developer does not collect, receive, or store any of your data. Everything the extension does happens either locally in your browser or as a direct connection from your browser to a third-party AI or translation service you choose and configure.

What gets sent, and to whom

1. The AI translation service you configure

To generate a translation, the extension sends:

  • the video's title and episode information,
  • the official subtitle text currently loaded by the video player, and
  • translation context built from that subtitle text (character names, relationships, honorific/address forms, and glossary terms)

directly from your browser to whichever AI service you have configured in Settings:

  • an API key you provide (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or any OpenAI-compatible server you specify),
  • a Codex/Claude/Gemini subscription, connected through a small local helper program that runs on your own computer, or
  • a local model you run yourself (e.g. LM Studio, Ollama) on your own machine or network.

In every case, this data goes straight from your browser (or your local helper program) to that service's own servers under that service's own privacy policy and terms. It never passes through, or is retained by, any server operated by this extension's developer — because there isn't one.

2. DeepL (optional machine-translation fill layer)

If you add a DeepL API key in Settings, the extension may also send subtitle text directly to DeepL's API — either as your chosen translation engine, or as a fast fill-in layer that covers upcoming lines while the AI backend catches up. This goes straight from your browser to DeepL under DeepL's own privacy policy and terms. DeepL is only contacted if you configure a key; otherwise this never happens.

3. Audio subtitles / speech-to-text (optional, per tab)

If a video has no usable subtitle track, you can start "audio subtitles" for that tab. Only after you explicitly start it, short chunks of that tab's audio are captured and sent to the speech-to-text endpoint you've configured in Settings — by default OpenAI's hosted Whisper API, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint you specify, including a self-hosted server. This never runs automatically or in the background, and only the tab you started it on is captured.

4. Public reference lookups for optional show context

When the extension recognizes a title, it may look up the title only (never your subtitle or dialogue text) on public reference sources — Wikidata, Wikipedia, and TVMaze — to enrich translation context with publicly available cast/character information. These are read-only, public lookups; no subtitle content is ever sent to them.

What never leaves your browser

  • Your settings (chosen AI service, target language, display preferences).
  • Generated and cached subtitle translations.
  • The reusable per-title translation context ("Series DB": character voice, relationships, glossary) built from your own viewing.
  • Any subtitle file you manually import from your own computer.

All of the above are stored locally in your browser's extension storage. The developer has no access to them, and they are never transmitted anywhere except as described in "What gets sent, and to whom" above.

What we don't do

  • We don't run a backend server.
  • We don't collect analytics, usage tracking, or advertising identifiers.
  • We don't sell or share your data with anyone, because we never receive it.

Your controls

  • You choose which AI service to use, and can switch or remove it at any time in Settings.
  • You can delete all cached/generated subtitles from Settings at any time.
  • Uninstalling the extension removes all locally stored data.
